## What I do

I’m a Senior Technical Animator at Tencent. I build locomotion, combat animation, character behaviors, transitions, and the systems that connect them to gameplay.

There are several categories of technical animation work, but one that stands out is catering to different scenarios and edge cases. For example, switching between movement states needs more than a set of good animations. The transitions also need to be designed carefully so the character stays responsive and the result does not feel repetitive.

## How I approach problems

I usually start broad. I map the situations a system needs to handle, then zone in on one specific case where the assumptions begin to break down.

That might be a transition that works at one speed but not another, a behavior interrupted by combat, or a character standing on a surface that is moving underneath them. The edge case becomes a way to understand the larger system.

I also like working close to both animation and engineering. On Assassin’s Creed Valhalla, I worked in a small group with one engineer and one animator. We rebuilt the system for the project instead of copying it from the previous game.

## Where I have worked

At Ubisoft, I worked on the Assassin’s Creed franchise and Skull & Bones. My work covered cinematics, gameplay animation, and animation systems for characters moving through very different kinds of environments.

At Tencent, I work on locomotion and combat systems for Monster Hunter Outlanders. The project is still in development, so the public details are intentionally limited.

## The other side of the work

I build side projects as a way to learn and immerse myself in AI. I learn best by doing, so I prefer starting with a real problem and using it to make the new technology concrete.

These projects are also where a more creative and playful side of my work shows up. Some are practical tools. Some are small games. The useful part is that each one gives me something specific to think through.
